# Development attribution and responsible AI use

Eternal Thread — Sovereign AI Demonstrator was created and is maintained by Nemi
Prowse as independent software development. Its development has included iterative
AI-assisted engineering support from OpenAI's Codex. That assistance can support
analysis, implementation drafts, testing, and documentation; the creator remains
responsible for the project's architecture, technical decisions, review, testing,
licensing, security posture, and publication.

## Open development without vendor dependence

This disclosure is intentional. A hosted AI development tool can help create an
open-source, local-first framework without becoming part of that framework's required
runtime. Eternal Thread is independently maintained and released under Apache-2.0. It
does not require OpenAI models, APIs, accounts, or services to run: operators choose
and manage their own compatible local inference runtime and models.

The project does not claim OpenAI partnership, funding, endorsement, certification,
or responsibility for the project. Mentioning Codex records a development tool used by
the creator; it is not a claim that Codex is the project's author or that the project
is vendor-approved.

## Verifiable project description

A concise description aligned with the public release materials is: an independent
developer designed and released an Apache-2.0, local-first framework for auditable
AI-assisted research with replaceable inference backends. The source code, tests,
design documentation, and release materials are the evidence for that description.

When the project is used as professional evidence, link to the relevant public
repository and release or tag, and describe only features that are present and
documented in that release. This keeps the authorship record clear while giving a
reviewer a direct way to verify the work.

## Responsible professional disclosure

When the project is described in a resume, portfolio, interview, or job application,
describe both the work and the assistance truthfully:

- identify Nemi Prowse as the creator and maintainer;
- describe the concrete work that can be explained and demonstrated, such as the
  local-first architecture, backend abstraction, privacy controls, tests,
  documentation, and release workflow;
- link to the relevant public repository and release or tag, and limit claims to
  what can be documented and supported with evidence in the referenced release;
- state that OpenAI Codex was used as an AI-assisted development tool when that
  context is relevant or requested;
- do not imply that the work was unaided, vendor-endorsed, government-certified, or
  reviewed by a hiring organization; and
- follow the specific instructions for every hiring process, assessment, and
  confidentiality requirement.

This document supports accurate professional disclosure; it does not replace a
resume, an application-specific explanation, or a candidate's responsibility to
explain their own work.

## Canadian federal public-service hiring context

The Public Service Commission of Canada (PSC) advises that hiring processes should
state whether candidates may use AI. Where a process permits AI, candidates may be
required to identify AI-generated content and its source and to explain their answers
in a follow-up interview. Where AI is prohibited, candidates must complete the
assessment independently. Job-posting and assessment instructions control in every
case; candidates must not submit protected assessment material to an AI tool.

The PSC also emphasizes transparency, validation of AI outputs, explainability,
human accountability, accessibility, and mitigation of bias and barriers in hiring.
This project's disclosure and evidence-oriented documentation are consistent with
those principles, but they do not constitute Government of Canada endorsement,
compliance certification, or an assessment of any candidate.

The broader AI Strategy for the Federal Public Service 2025-2027 similarly frames
responsible AI adoption around public trust, transparency, and accountable use. This
project is an independent open-source reference implementation, not a Government of
Canada product or service.
